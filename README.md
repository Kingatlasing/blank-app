# ⛏️ Minecraft Scale Model Designer

Design accurate, scaled Minecraft block models of real-world landmarks — or literally anything else — with computed measurements, a hollow-or-filled interior, modeled interior chambers/floors, a layer-by-layer building blueprint, and ready-to-paste `/fill` build commands.

This is a self-contained HTML/CSS/JS app (`app/index.html`, rendered with [three.js](https://threejs.org/) for the 3D preview) shown full-screen through a thin Streamlit wrapper (`streamlit_app.py`). No backend, no build step — everything runs client-side in the browser.

## What it does

Ask it (by picking from the library, or describing a shape) for something like *"a 3/8 scale replica of Khufu's Pyramid, filled, with the interior chambers"* and it will:

- **Compute real measurements** — real-world height/footprint, your chosen scale ratio (e.g. `3/8`, or "target height in blocks"), and the resulting size in Minecraft blocks.
- **Generate the 3D shape** as a voxel model, textured with real Minecraft block art and previewed live in the browser.
- **Model the interior** — for the Great Pyramid (and the other Giza pyramids), it carves a proportionally-scaled entrance, descending/ascending passages, Grand Gallery, and King's & Queen's Chambers based on published Giza figures, lit with torches. For towers, houses, and other buildings, it hollows the shape and adds floor slabs, a ladder-equipped stairwell, a real door at the entrance, and windows — with textured doors, glass panes, ladders, and torches, not placeholder blocks.
- **Toggle hollow vs. filled** — a solid core with an exterior skin (realistic, more blocks) or a thin shell (lighter, faster to build).
- **Let you explore the inside** — orbit, a free-fly camera, a first-person walk mode, or third-person, all with real gravity/collision and Minecraft's own camera scale (1.62 block eye height, 0.6×1.8 hitbox, 70° FOV), plus a cutaway slider that clips away one side to see inside without switching modes.
- **Let you actually operate the mechanisms** — turn on Interact mode and click any door, trapdoor, lever, button, or pressure plate to see it open/close or flip, with a real hinge-pivoted swing animation, not a texture swap. Walking onto a pressure plate in Walk mode triggers it too. Redstone power is a real simulation, not a lookup table: a lever/button/plate is a power source, a torch genuinely inverts based on the block it's stuck to (goes dark when that block is powered, strongly powers the block on its far side when lit — the real vanilla mechanic every logic gate below is built from), and redstone wire carries a signal outward with vanilla's real 15-level falloff and auto-connects into a cross shape toward whatever's actually next to it — right-click a wire to trim one of its side connections (the same real vanilla feature, so two lines can run one cell apart without merging).
- **Include a real redstone logic-gate and contraption library** — NOT, AND, OR, NAND, NOR, XOR, XNOR, a signal booster, and an RS NOR Latch (the first with real memory, powered by the simulation's tick-based state persistence), each a genuinely simulated circuit (not a scripted animation) you can flip the lever(s) on and watch respond, plus a Two-Key AND Door, a Pitfall Trap, and a Timed Button Door showing gates and wire driving real mechanisms — the last one also demonstrates a wooden button's real vanilla auto-release timer. Search by name or description ("AND gate", "hidden door trap", "self closing door") right from the structure picker.
- **Place rails and a minecart** — straight, curved, and ascending rail (plus powered, detector, and activator variants) with real vanilla geometry (the ascending tilt matches the actual block model's 45° rotation), and a minecart that rests on top.
- **Build absolutely anything, block by block** — pick "Freeform" as the custom shape for a blank buildable platform, turn on Build mode, and left-click any face to place the selected block there or right-click a block to remove it, exactly like Minecraft's own creative placement. There's no shape library for a Thanos gauntlet, an X-wing, or a Hogwarts tower — but with every one of the ~320 real blocks available and full manual placement, you can build any of them yourself, then export the result with the same `/fill`/`/setblock` pipeline as everything else.
- **Show a layer-by-layer blueprint** — a scrubbable top-down view of every Y-level, color-coded by block type, with door/window/torch/ladder/lever/plate markers, so you know exactly what to place where.
- **Export build commands** — optimized (rectangle-merged) `/fill` commands plus `/setblock` commands for doors, windows, torches, levers, buttons, and plates, as a downloadable `.mcfunction` file.
- **Report full stats** — block counts by material, total blocks, stack/shulker-box counts, and notes on every simplification made.

### Real Minecraft block textures, real Minecraft geometry

Every texture (blocks, doors, windows, torches, levers, buttons, tripwire, plants) is the actual in-game art, not a redrawn approximation, sourced from [PrismarineJS/minecraft-assets](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/minecraft-assets) — a community project that extracts textures from the Minecraft client for use by tools like mineflayer and prismarine-viewer — and embedded directly in `app/index.html` (along with a vendored copy of three.js) so the page needs no network access at all. These are Mojang's own copyrighted art assets, unofficially re-published; using them this way is a long-standing, widely-tolerated norm in the Minecraft tooling community, but it's worth knowing if this project is ever redistributed publicly rather than kept for personal use.

The block picker covers **321 real blocks** — every full-cube block in the game (all wood types, stone/deepslate/nether/end variants, ores, all 16 colors of wool/concrete/terracotta/glazed-terracotta/stained-glass, copper oxidation stages, coral, and more), extracted programmatically from Minecraft's own block-model data rather than hand-picked, grouped by category in the dropdown.

Non-cube elements are modeled as real 3D volumes with real measurements (in 16ths of a block, matching Minecraft's own modeling unit), not flat sprites or texture swaps:

- **Doors** — two full-height 16×16×3px panels, correctly flush against one edge of the block (not centered) and pivoting around the true hinge edge, so opening is a real 90° swing you can watch happen.
- **Trapdoors** — a 16×16×3px panel mounted flush at the floor or ceiling, swinging to vertical against the wall when opened.
- **Levers** — a genuine two-part object (6×3×5px stone base + 2×10×2px arm) that mounts on and orients to the floor, a wall, or the ceiling, with the arm's angle changing between on/off.
- **Buttons** and **pressure plates** — real proportions (6×2×4px; 14×14×1px inset from the block edges) that visibly depress when pressed.
- **Torches** (regular/soul/redstone) — a standing 3×3×10px rod, not a billboard cross; redstone torches swap between lit/unlit textures.
- **Tripwire + hooks**, a **chair** (the actual L-shaped stair-block silhouette), and cross-plane **flowers/grass/fire** and a simple **tree** (log trunk + leaf canopy) round out the non-cube set.
- **Rails and a minecart** — straight/curved/ascending track (plus powered, detector, and activator rail) built from real vanilla block-model geometry, and an open-hull minecart. There's no rail art in this app's texture atlas, so rails render as plain colored metal-rail-and-wood-tie geometry (vanilla's real colour cues — iron, gold, red-orange — still apply) rather than a texture.

These are placed automatically by the generators — torches lighting the Great Pyramid's passages, a tripwire-and-lever trap flourish near its entrance, and a working door/windows/ladder/chair set in generic building interiors — and there's a whole **Traps & Mechanisms** and **Redstone Logic Gates** category of dedicated showcase presets (see below).

### Redstone Logic Gates library

Ten presets — **Input/Output** (the trivial baseline: a lever powering adjacent dust directly, no torch — output just is the input), **NOT**, **Signal Booster** (a buffer/repeater equivalent), **OR**, **NAND**, **NOR**, **AND**, **XOR**, **XNOR**, and **RS NOR Latch** — each a real, physically-buildable vanilla circuit, not a hardcoded truth table. Every one is built from exactly two genuine mechanics: a torch inverting the block it's mounted on (and strongly powering the block on its far side when lit), and redstone dust merging (a dust cell powered by whichever of its neighbors is on, for free OR logic with no torch at all). AND/NAND/NOR are torch-inverted compositions of those two (De Morgan's laws — AND = NOT(NAND), NAND = OR(NOT a, NOT b), NOR = NOT(OR(a,b))), exactly like a real redstone tutorial would build them. XOR is the hard one, same as it is for real redstone builders: it's AND(OR(a,b), NAND(a,b)), and combining those two already-composed signals means routing power between "wire power" and "block power" more than once, which is also why it needed one more genuine mechanic added to the simulation — a block powered by a switch or a lit torch's target also charges dust resting directly on it (real vanilla), letting a signal cross back into a same-height wire run instead of staying stuck at whatever height the circuit that produced it happened to leave it at. XNOR is XOR's circuit with one more inverting torch on the end. Every gate's truth table is checked against all 4 input combinations, run through the actual generated preset (not just the abstract circuit), by this repo's own test suite before being wired in. Turn on Interact mode and flip the lever(s) — the torches genuinely relight or go dark, and the output wire responds because the simulation actually recomputed it, not because a script set a color.

**RS NOR Latch** is the first gate in the library with real memory: two NOR gates cross-coupled so each one's output feeds the other's input (Qp = NOT(S OR Q), Q = NOT(R OR Qp)). This only works because the simulation now persists its block-power state across separate `computeRedstone` calls rather than starting fresh every time (see "Tick-based simulation" below) — without that, a torch's lit state can never depend on what the *other* torch in the loop settled on a moment ago, which is exactly what a latch needs to hold state at all. Pulse S (Set) on then off and Q stays lit; pulse R (Reset) on then off and Q goes dark and stays there — flipping both levers back off doesn't erase either result, matching a real Minecraft RS NOR latch.

Three more presets show gates and wire driving a real mechanism instead of just lighting a wire: **Two-Key AND Door** (a door wired straight into the AND gate's own output cell — both levers have to be on before it's powered), **Pitfall Trap** (a pressure plate wired through a real redstone_wire run — real falloff and everything — to a trapdoor pit), and **Timed Button Door** (a wooden button wired straight to a door — see "Component timing rules" below for what makes this one different from the other two). The older **Redstone Mechanism Demo Room** (a lever wired straight to the entrance door and a pressure plate wired straight to a floor-trapdoor pit) still uses a simpler, deliberate 1:1 trigger link rather than the real propagation, for its own more basic demonstration; **Redstone & Rails Workshop** combines the real propagation with every rail type and a minecart.

### Component timing rules

Buttons now follow their real vanilla timing instead of behaving like a lever with no memory. A wooden button (any `*_planks` tile) stays pressed for 15 redstone ticks (1.5 real seconds); a stone-family button (stone, polished blackstone) for 10 redstone ticks (1 real second) — both real Minecraft values, source: [minecraft.wiki/w/Button](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Button). It releases itself on that real-time timer with no further input, and — also matching real vanilla — clicking a button that's already pressed does nothing; the only way to release one early in real Minecraft is a fired arrow, which this app doesn't simulate. Exported `/setblock` commands for a button also now name its real block id (`oak_button`, `stone_button`, `polished_blackstone_button`, …) based on its own material instead of always saying `oak_button`. **Timed Button Door** is the one preset built around this — every other preset that needs a switch uses a lever or a pressure plate instead, since those don't have a real auto-release timer to show off.

### Block property rules

Five full-cube materials now follow their real vanilla physics or appearance instead of behaving like inert stone:

- **Gravity-affected blocks** — sand, red sand, gravel, and every concrete powder color fall straight down through any air beneath them and settle on the first solid block (or the floor), exactly like real vanilla's falling-block update, whenever a Build-mode edit could have left one unsupported. A stack of several separated by gaps compacts down together in order, the same end-state real vanilla's block-by-block falling reaches. This intentionally only runs after a Build-mode edit, never after generating a preset or custom shape — a real Minecraft world would just as happily let a hollow sand pyramid collapse the moment it's placed, but that's the opposite of what a *scale model* generator is for, so presets and custom shapes are placed as designed rather than physically re-simulated.
- **Redstone lamp** — lights up (swaps to its lit texture) the instant it's adjacent to a powered wire, switch, or torch target, and goes dark again the instant it isn't, using the same real signal propagation as every other consumer in this app (see "How the redstone simulation works" below), just checked in all 6 directions instead of the 4 a same-height wire uses, matching how a real redstone lamp can be lit from above or below too.
- **Soul sand / soul soil** — slow the player down by ~41.91% while standing on top, matching real vanilla's actual figure for anyone not wearing Soul Speed boots (source: [minecraft.wiki/w/Soul_Sand](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Soul_Sand); this app has no boots or enchantments, so it's unconditional). This works in every camera mode that walks — Walk and Third-person — checked live against the real block directly under the player's feet each frame.
- **Ice / packed ice / blue ice** — carry real momentum instead of the player snapping straight to a stop or a new direction: releasing a movement key on ice keeps sliding for a while, and blue ice (the game's most slippery block, real slipperiness 0.989) slides noticeably farther than regular ice (0.98), which in turn slides farther than packed ice (0.95) — matching real vanilla's own ordering (source: [minecraft.wiki/w/Slipperiness](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Slipperiness)). This app's baseline walk speed was never derived from vanilla's exact per-tick friction/acceleration formula (it's a flat blocks/second constant, not a physics simulation), so rather than bolt that formula onto a different speed model, the slide strength is a qualitative match scaled by how much *more* slippery each variant is than normal ground — it reduces to exactly the old instant movement on anything that isn't ice, so normal-ground movement is unaffected.
- **Glass and every stained-glass color** — render genuinely see-through in the 3D preview instead of a solid opaque cube, matching real vanilla glass. The source texture atlas isn't a transparent PNG (vanilla's own glass textures are opaque pixels rendered on a separate transparent render layer, not via per-pixel alpha), so this is a flat reduced material opacity rather than an alpha-channel effect — real vanilla **tinted glass** is the one deliberate exception, since it "blocks all light" and is documented as rendering opaque, so it's excluded and stays a solid cube like every other block.

All five were previously in the block palette (Build mode could already place any of them) but had no real behavior wired up — placing sand just left it floating, a redstone lamp never lit no matter what was next to it, soul sand was exactly as fast to cross as stone, ice was exactly as grippy as stone too, and glass looked exactly as solid as stone.

Not yet covered: slime block and honey block (bouncing, climbing, slowed fall) aren't in this app's block palette at all yet.

### Tick-based simulation

`computeRedstone` now persists its block-power state (`model._blockPoweredExternal`) on the model object itself between calls, not just between passes inside one call. A settling circuit (any of the combinational gates above) still resolves in one call using several internal passes, exactly as before; a *stateful* circuit like the RS NOR Latch additionally relies on that state surviving across genuinely separate calls, which is what makes real per-tick simulation possible — driving the same model through repeated single-pass `computeRedstone(model, 1)` calls (e.g. on a timer) now behaves like a real sequence of redstone ticks instead of resetting the circuit's history every time. This is also the foundation a self-oscillating clock/pulser preset would need (a NOT loop that flips every tick) — not yet included as a preset, since nothing in the UI drives a live tick loop over real time yet, but the underlying simulation now supports it.

### Fluid flow simulation (water and lava)

Water used to just be a decor marker hand-painted onto whichever cells a preset wanted "wet" — nothing stopped it from covering an entire flat floor with no source in sight. `computeWaterFlow` replaces that with a real flood-fill from explicit source cells (`type: 'water', source: true`), following the same *shape* of rule real vanilla water does (source: [minecraft.wiki/w/Water](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Water)): a source falls straight down through open air as an unbroken column, and once it's actually resting on something solid (the floor, or wherever a fall lands), it spreads outward — thinning by one level each block — up to a limited distance (`WATER_MAX_LEVEL = 7`, matching real vanilla's own spread limit) before stopping. A cell with no real path back to a source is never water at all; a preset that wants a pond places a handful of real sources, the same way a player fills one with water buckets, rather than every cell being painted directly.

That limited spread has one real exception, and it's a big one: a hole genuinely walled in on all 4 horizontal sides doesn't stay limited to a short puddle. Real vanilla water has a "spring" mechanic — two flowing water blocks that meet next to an existing source convert into a fresh source themselves — which is how a player actually fills an entire tank from just one or two buckets rather than needing a source in every corner. `enclosedWaterPocket` checks whether a source's connected pocket of open air ever touches the model's outer x/z edge; an open top is fine (that's just the container's rim), but if the sides never escape, the *whole* pocket becomes water, however large, not a 7-block radius. The Aquarium Chamber's single source genuinely fills its entire glass tank this way. The Sea Turtle Beach preset's ocean, by contrast, is open on 3 of its sides (it runs to the edge of the model, like real open water), so it correctly keeps the limited spread instead — and it needed an actual elevation step between the dry sand and the shallows for the same underlying reason: on one flat, unenclosed floor, real water would just spread across the whole thing (correctly — that's what it does in real vanilla too), so the beach half is genuinely one block higher than the ocean floor, a real solid boundary instead of an invisible line.

Water also finally moves. A falling column sweeps with a real looping downward motion (the closest stand-in this app can manage for vanilla's scrolling waterfall texture, since it has no scrolling-texture support), while resting water gets a small animated ripple instead of sitting perfectly still — driven by its own per-frame `updateWaterAnimations` loop, separate from the mob AI loop. The squid and sea turtle's own water checks (`squidCellSwimmable`, reused by both) now query these real computed cells instead of a hand-placed list, and the squid's target-picking was changed to only ever move along one axis at a time, so it can't cut diagonally through a dry corner between two cells that are each individually real water — a real seam an irregularly-shaped body of water can expose that a solid filled cube never did.

The whole engine (`computeWaterFlow`) was generalized into `computeFluidFlow` to add real lava, since lava follows the identical *shape* of rule with exactly two real, documented differences: it spreads a shorter distance (`LAVA_MAX_LEVEL = 4`, matching real vanilla's roughly 4-block Nether spread, versus water's 7), and it has none of water's "spring" mechanic — a lava lake dropped into the exact same fully-walled container that fills water to the brim stays a limited puddle instead, because real vanilla lava never converts two flowing blocks into a fresh source the way water does. `computeLavaFlow` calls the same shared function with `allowEnclosedFill: false`, and a dedicated test (`lava-flow-test.js`) builds the identical enclosed room twice — once with each fluid — specifically to prove that one fills completely and the other doesn't.

### Farms & Automation

Two presets — **Automatic Wheat Farm** and **Animal Breeding Pen** — the one deliberate place mobs/animals show up in this app; the general walk-around 3D view is otherwise intentionally mob-free. Neither farmland, water, wheat, fences, hoppers, chests, nor any mob/animal model exist in this app's texture atlas or decor set, so both presets are built from honestly-labeled stand-ins (spelled out in each preset's own description/notes): the wheat farm's water-flow harvest layout (crop rows flanking a central channel, collected by a hopper at the downstream end — real vanilla farmland stays tilled within 4 blocks of water, and a harvested crop floats to a collection point on flowing water) uses a chosen block for "farmland" and another for "water," and grass_tuft decor standing in for planted wheat; the hopper itself is real new procedural geometry (a basin over a spout, no texture existed for the real tapered shape either). The animal pen uses the existing iron_bars decor as a stand-in fence and new simple low-poly cow/pig/sheep/chicken/rabbit models (a few coloured boxes each) rather than real (textured, animated) mobs. Both show the correct real-world layout/mechanism, not a literal 1:1 reproduction of the missing art.

A third preset, **Creeper Farm**, adds this app's first genuinely AUTOMATIC production chain: a real spawner cage block periodically spawns a real, live creeper, which is automatically killed a short beat later with nobody anywhere nearby, dropping real gunpowder at a hopper on the far end — real vanilla mob farms work exactly this way, a spawner periodically producing a mob that's automatically killed by some passive method (a long fall, drowning, etc.) with its drop funneled by water into a hopper, entirely unattended ([minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorials/Mob_farm](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorials/Mob_farm)). Every earlier item-producing interaction in this app needs either a direct player click (shearing a mooshroom) or a player's own live proximity (a piglin noticing a dropped gold ingot); a mob farm needs neither. A new `mob_spawner` decor entry — purely a logic marker with no geometry of its own, since the real spawner cage block already sits in the grid as an ordinary material — tracks its own real spawn timer every frame via `updateMobSpawner`, waiting if its own small cap of currently-alive farm-spawned mobs is already full (the same real "won't spawn if too crowded" vanilla rule), and `spawnFarmMob` creates a genuine, fully live mob entry the instant it fires, tagged `_farmSpawned`. Two new GENERIC checks sit at the very top of `updateMobs`' own per-frame loop, ahead of the ordinary per-type dispatch and not specific to creepers at all: one automatically calls `killFarmMob` once any `_farmSpawned` mob's own real kill timer runs out (this app's honest stand-in for a real kill method, since it has no combat or health system to simulate an actual one), dropping its own configured loot at the farm's own drop point; the other automatically removes any `_farmCollect` dropped item once its own real collection timer runs out, simulating a hopper sucking it up. Because both generic checks key off tags on the entry rather than its type, any future farm mob just needs to be spawned through this same path to get identical automatic kill/collect behavior for free. Verified directly by a unit test confirming no creeper or gunpowder exists before the spawner's own real timer first elapses, that a genuine live creeper appears the instant it does, that it's automatically removed and replaced by real gunpowder at the hopper once its own real kill timer runs out with nobody nearby, and that the gunpowder is itself automatically removed once its own real collection timer runs out, plus a second test confirming the spawner genuinely stops spawning once its own real active-mob cap is reached. This app has no fluid-current or fall-physics system at all, so the mob's own trip from spawn point to kill point and the item's own trip into the hopper are both honestly stood in for by fixed timers rather than a simulated fall or water current, and this app has no verified real spawner delay, active-mob cap, or kill/collection timing, so the figures used are honest, approximate stand-ins, the same caveat already applied throughout this app's other compressed timers. Not modeled: a real creeper's own chance-based gunpowder count (0-2, boosted by Looting — this always drops exactly one), spawner activation only within a real player's own nearby range, and the real light-level requirements a creeper actually needs to spawn at all.

### Mob AI rules

Five low-poly stand-in animals — cow, pig, sheep, chicken, and rabbit, the only mobs this app has any geometry for — now genuinely wander instead of standing frozen in place. All five real animals ([minecraft.wiki](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Cow)) document the same base behavior: idle for a while, then pick a random spot nearby and walk to it, never stepping through an obstacle or off a ledge. This app's animals follow the same idle → pick-a-spot → walk → idle loop in real time in the 3D view (any camera mode, not just while Interact/Build mode is on), checking every candidate step against the actual grid and against the pen's real iron_bars fence decor so none of them can wander through a wall or the fence, or step off the floor into open air. It's a genuine shared per-frame simulation, not a scripted animation loop, and a new mob would only need to register itself (`registerMob`) and opt into the same `MOB_KINDS` set to get it for free.

They also pick up real vanilla's other documented idle behavior: a cow "looks at players within six blocks for two to four seconds sometimes." The same `LookAtPlayerGoal` is a generic goal shared by most vanilla mobs, so this app applies it to all five rather than cow alone — though the exact 2-4 second duration is confirmed specifically for cows and extended to the others by reasonable analogy, not independently sourced per species. Whenever an animal is idle (an active wander goal always wins, matching real vanilla's own goal priority) and the camera is in Walk or Third-person mode — the only modes with an actual player position in the world, since Orbit and Fly are editor viewpoints rather than something standing in the scene — a nearby animal will occasionally turn to face the player for a real few seconds before going back to whatever it was doing. This app has no per-tick random-number model to match vanilla's exact trigger rate, so the "sometimes" is an approximated, occasional trigger chance rather than the literal vanilla formula, but the range, the duration, and the going-back-to-idle-afterward are all real.

The rabbit is the one animal that doesn't just reuse the shared walk cycle as-is: real rabbits "hop around aimlessly instead of walking" (same source), so while it uses the exact same idle/pick-a-spot/walk/idle state machine as the other four, its walking phase adds a real visible bounce (an up-and-down arc timed to its stride) instead of a smooth glide, and it covers ground faster per real vanilla's quicker hops.

Not modeled for any of the five, since none of it has any foothold in this app yet: breeding, being attracted to a player holding their specific food (wheat for cow/sheep, carrots/potatoes/beetroot for pig, seeds for chicken, carrots/golden carrots/dandelions for rabbit), fleeing when hurt, or avoiding water — no inventory/held-item concept, no "hurt" concept, no water fluid. Faking any of those would be a cosmetic flourish, not a real rule the way the wander loop is.

**Bat** is the sixth mob, and a genuinely different kind of behavior rather than another ground-walker: real bats ([minecraft.wiki/w/Bat](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Bat)) "hang upside down on the underside of a solid block when idle" and "often fly around aimlessly" the rest of the time, fleeing if a player gets close while roosting. This app's bat does exactly that — roosts (rendered genuinely upside down) under a real solid ceiling block, occasionally flies off in real 3D (not just x/z) to a new roost elsewhere in the room, and flushes into flight early if you (in Walk or Third-person mode) get close enough while it's hanging. It needed its own small state machine (`updateBat`) rather than reusing the ground-mob one above, since a bat needs a ceiling instead of a floor and moves in three dimensions instead of two. It's also the reason the new **Bat Cave Chamber** preset exists: the Animal Breeding Pen has no ceiling at all for a bat to hang from, and forcing one into a farm pen it doesn't belong in would have been worse than giving it a real (if minimal) home.

Real vanilla goes further still: bats only spawn "in dark places, in any solid block below a full light level of 4." This app has no actual light-propagation engine to check a precise level against, so it approximates "dark" as "no light-emitting block in or immediately touching this cell" — every light-emitting full-cube material in the palette (glowstone, sea lantern, shroomlight, all three froglight colors, a lit redstone lamp), plus the always-lit torch/soul_torch decor and a redstone_torch only while it's actually lit. A bat will never settle on a roost that fails this check, and — since the Bat Cave Chamber's exterior/interior/accent materials are all user-choosable — a bat that's already roosting also re-checks its own spot every frame and flees the instant it stops being dark, the same as if a player had walked up to it. Pick glowstone as the cave's accent block and watch the bat immediately take off looking for somewhere dark, exactly like a real one would refuse to roost there at all.

**Enderman** is the seventh mob, and reuses the shared ground-mob wander loop almost as-is — with two deliberate, real exceptions rather than a straight copy. Real endermen "wander and/or teleport around randomly" ([minecraft.wiki/w/Enderman](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Enderman)): every time this app's enderman picks a new spot to go, there's roughly a coin-flip chance it teleports there instantly instead of walking, exactly matching that real mix of the two. And it never enters the look-at-player state the other six share — real vanilla actually has this backwards for endermen: the *player* looking at *it* is what triggers aggression, not the reverse, and this app has no way to know which direction the camera is actually pointed (only where it is), so faking that trigger would be a guess dressed up as a rule, not a real one. It lives on the new **End Islands Flourish** preset (a small floating end-stone-and-purpur platform), since real vanilla endermen are The End's signature mob. No aggro or attack behavior is modeled at all — this app has no combat system of any kind — so this enderman is calm and never actually turns hostile.

**Spider** is the eighth mob, and adds a real movement mode none of the other seven have: real vanilla spiders "can climb up on the sides of solid blocks," and this is documented as happening even while a spider is just wandering randomly, not only while chasing something — "unique to spiders" among mobs ([minecraft.wiki/w/Spider](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Spider)). This app's spider shares the exact same idle/pick-a-spot ground wander as every other mob, but whenever it's actually touching a wall, it has a real chance to climb a few blocks up (or down) that wall instead of picking a normal floor destination — height changes, horizontal position stays pinned to the wall, and it visibly pitches flat against the surface while doing it. It needed a genuine third state (`climbing`, alongside idle/walking) and a live per-frame check for whether the spider is actually against a wall (`hasAdjacentWall`), since nothing else in this app moves vertically along a surface it's touching. Lives in a new **Spider Nest** preset — the first fully 4-walled room among the mob presets, since climbing needs a real vertical wall the open-sided animal pen and bat cave don't have. Real vanilla's light-level-based aggression (hostile in the dark, neutral in bright light) isn't modeled, since it only affects whether a spider attacks, and this app has no combat system at all.

### How the redstone simulation works

Levers, buttons, and pressure plates emit power (level 15) while switched on. Redstone torches genuinely invert: a torch goes dark the instant the block it's mounted on is powered, and a lit torch strongly powers the block on its far side — a real mechanic computed live (via a small fixed-point iteration, so chains of inverters settle correctly), not a static flag. A block powered by a switch or a lit torch's target also charges any redstone wire resting directly on it (real vanilla), which is what lets a signal move between "block power" and "wire power" more than once in a single circuit — the mechanic the XOR/XNOR gates above need and the simpler gates don't. Redstone wire carries a signal outward from any source cell it touches, losing exactly 1 power level per block — the same falloff real redstone dust uses, so a source 16+ blocks from a component through unbroken wire genuinely won't reach it — and only through sides that are actually enabled: right-clicking a wire (Interact mode) trims one of its four side connections at a time, in real vanilla fashion, letting two lines run past each other one cell apart without merging. Any door, trapdoor, powered rail, or activator rail sitting next to a powered wire or source cell picks up power automatically; there's no per-object "wired to" table involved unless a preset's generator explicitly hardcodes one (the original Mechanism Demo Room still does, for its own simpler demonstration). The block-power state each pass computes is now persisted on the model between separate `computeRedstone` calls too, not just between passes within one call — a settling circuit still resolves in one call (several internal passes), but this is what lets a genuinely stateful circuit like the RS NOR Latch (see above) remember its last Set/Reset across real, separate interactions instead of recomputing from a blank slate every time. What isn't modeled: repeaters/comparators as placeable components, and signal strength through solid blocks (only through wire) — real feedback buses in this app instead route through short adjacent-dust runs (kept within the same 15-level falloff budget real redstone dust has). A self-oscillating clock/pulser isn't in the gate library yet either, since nothing in the UI drives a live tick loop over real time — the persistence needed for one now exists in the simulation, just not a preset or a running clock built on top of it.

### Honest limitations

This app ships with a curated library of real dimensions and a handful of geometric primitives — it doesn't have live internet access or an AI model looking up arbitrary buildings on demand. Interior layouts (especially the pyramid passages) are proportional approximations based on published figures, not surveyed blueprints, and very thin real-world features (like a 1.2m-wide passage) get clamped to a minimum of 1 Minecraft block. Each generated model's *Measurements & Stats* tab lists the specific simplifications made for that structure. Curved rail is a stylized diagonal stand-in for vanilla's real quarter-circle artwork, not a literal curve — there was no way to reproduce that specific piece of pixel art without the source texture, which isn't in this app's atlas.

### Not in this pass yet

A guided freeform *generator* for whole categories (describe a fortress with traps, or a maze, and have it auto-built) is a natural next step, but it's a fundamentally different feature from the direct block-by-block Build mode this pass added — that one lets you build anything by hand right now. Repeaters/comparators as real placeable components, and a self-oscillating clock/pulser preset built on the tick-based persistence the RS NOR Latch already uses, are the natural next extensions to the gate library and propagation simulation above. A mob grinder/spawner-based farm is the natural next farm preset once there's a second mob model to justify it.
